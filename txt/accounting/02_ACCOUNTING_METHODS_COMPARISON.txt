================================================================================
                        ACCOUNTING METHODS COMPARISON
                    Which Method is Right for SweetTooth?
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Three main accounting methods exist. SweetTooth currently implements a HYBRID
PERPETUAL ACCRUAL model, which is the gold standard for retail + production.


METHOD 1: CASH BASIS ACCOUNTING
================================================================================

Description:
  Revenue and expenses recorded only when CASH changes hands
  
Timing of Recording:
  Revenue:   When customer pays (not when sale is made)
  Expenses:  When payment is made (not when bill is received)
  Inventory: No accrual; immediate expense when purchased

Advantages:
  ✓ Simplest to implement
  ✓ Clear cash position
  ✓ Minimal bookkeeping
  ✓ Ideal for small, cash-only businesses

Disadvantages:
  ✗ Distorts true profitability (especially with credit)
  ✗ No inventory tracking during period
  ✗ Poor for planning and analysis
  ✗ Not compliant with GAAP/IFRS
  ✗ Not suitable for businesses with significant inventory

Suitable For:
  - Very small businesses (1-2 employees)
  - Cash-only businesses
  - Service businesses (no inventory)
  - Sole proprietors

NOT SUITABLE FOR SWEETTOOTH because:
  ✗ Multi-branch requires accurate real-time inventory
  ✗ Production requires recipe costing (which needs inventory)
  ✗ Retail needs to track shelf inventory vs paid amounts
  ✗ Cannot determine true profitability


METHOD 2: ACCRUAL BASIS ACCOUNTING (PERPETUAL)
================================================================================

Description:
  Revenue and expenses recorded when earned/incurred, regardless of cash timing
  WITH real-time inventory tracking
  
Timing of Recording:
  Revenue:   When sale is made (customer takes goods)
  Expenses:  When invoice is received (not paid)
  Inventory: Updated in real-time with every movement

Advantages:
  ✓ Accurate profitability measurement
  ✓ Real-time inventory visibility
  ✓ Required for GAAP/IFRS
  ✓ Excellent for multi-branch operations
  ✓ Supports production costing
  ✓ Works with credit sales/purchases
  ✓ Better for financial analysis
  ✓ Automated COGS calculation

Disadvantages:
  ✗ More complex implementation
  ✗ Higher bookkeeping volume
  ✗ Requires good controls
  ✗ Timing differences between cash and profit

Suitable For:
  ✓ Medium to large businesses
  ✓ Businesses with inventory
  ✓ Multi-branch operations
  ✓ Production businesses
  ✓ Companies needing accurate financial reporting

PERFECT FOR SWEETTOOTH because:
  ✓ Multi-branch inventory tracking needed
  ✓ Production requires recipe costing
  ✓ POS needs real-time stock updates
  ✓ Accurate profit = accurate branch performance
  ✓ Already partially implemented (Stock.average_cost)


METHOD 3: ACCRUAL BASIS ACCOUNTING (PERIODIC)
================================================================================

Description:
  Revenue and expenses recorded when earned/incurred
  Inventory counted and valued ONLY at period-end (month/year)
  
Timing of Recording:
  Revenue:   When sale is made
  Expenses:  When invoice is received
  Inventory: Counted manually at period-end; COGS calculated then

Advantages:
  ✓ Simpler than perpetual accrual
  ✓ Less frequent transactions
  ✓ Lower technology requirements
  ✓ Good compromise for cost

Disadvantages:
  ✗ No real-time inventory visibility
  ✗ Stock-outs discovered late
  ✗ Cannot calculate COGS until period-end
  ✗ Not suitable for multiple branches
  ✗ Poor for production planning
  ✗ Reconciliation challenges

Suitable For:
  - Small retail stores
  - Simple businesses without production
  - Businesses that can close for inventory count

NOT SUITABLE FOR SWEETTOOTH because:
  ✗ Multi-branch needs real-time inventory
  ✗ Production requires ongoing costing
  ✗ POS integration requires live stock updates


METHOD 4: MODIFIED ACCRUAL (Government/Non-Profit)
================================================================================

Description:
  Revenue recognized when available and measurable
  Expenses recognized when liability incurred
  Used by governments and non-profits
  
NOT SUITABLE FOR SWEETTOOTH - This is for government/non-profit entities

INVENTORY COSTING METHOD: WEIGHTED AVERAGE
================================================================================

Why Weighted Average (not FIFO/LIFO)?
  ✓ Currently implemented in Stock.updateAverageCost()
  ✓ Reduces volatility from price fluctuations
  ✓ Fairest representation of actual costs
  ✓ Allowed under IFRS
  ✓ Simpler than FIFO for multi-location
  ✗ LIFO not allowed under IFRS (good reason to avoid)

Formula:
  New Average Cost = ((Old Qty × Old Avg Cost) + (New Qty × New Cost)) / Total Qty

Example:
  Current inventory: 100 units @ 50 NGN = 5,000 NGN value
  New purchase:     50 units @ 60 NGN = 3,000 NGN cost
  Total inventory:  150 units
  New avg cost = (5,000 + 3,000) / 150 = 53.33 NGN per unit


JOURNAL ENTRIES FOR PERPETUAL ACCRUAL
================================================================================

Entry 1: SALE TRANSACTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Scenario: Customer buys 2 units of Product X
  - Cost price: 50 NGN per unit
  - Selling price: 100 NGN per unit
  - Sales tax: 7.5%
  - Sale total: 215 NGN (100*2 + 15 tax)

Entry A: Record Sale Revenue
  Date: Sale completion date
  
  Account                          Debit      Credit
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Cash/Bank/Receivable             215
      Sales Revenue                           200
      Sales Tax Payable                        15
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (To record sale of 2 units at 100 NGN)

Entry B: Record COGS (Perpetual System - Automatic)
  Date: Same as sale
  
  Account                          Debit      Credit
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Cost of Goods Sold               100
      Inventory                               100
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (To record COGS for 2 units at 50 NGN average cost)

Net Effect: Profit = 200 (revenue) - 100 (COGS) = 100 NGN (before tax expense)


Entry 2: PURCHASE TRANSACTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Scenario: Purchase 50 units @ 40 NGN + 500 NGN shipping
  - FOB cost: 2,000 NGN (50 × 40)
  - Other costs: 500 NGN (shipping)
  - Total cost: 2,500 NGN (landing cost)
  - Cost per unit: 50 NGN

Entry C: Record Purchase (On Approval)
  Date: Purchase approval date
  
  Account                          Debit      Credit
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Inventory                        2,500
      Accounts Payable                     2,500
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (To record purchase of 50 units with landing cost of 2,500 NGN)

Note: Shipping allocated proportionally across items
      50 units share 500 NGN = 10 NGN per unit
      Cost per unit = 40 + 10 = 50 NGN

Entry D: Record Payment (When Cash Paid)
  Date: Payment date (may differ from purchase date)
  
  Account                          Debit      Credit
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Accounts Payable                 2,500
      Cash/Bank                               2,500
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (To record payment for purchase)


Entry 3: INVENTORY ADJUSTMENT (DAMAGE/SHRINKAGE)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Scenario: 5 units damaged (cost 50 NGN per unit = 250 NGN)

Entry E: Record Damage
  Date: Damage discovery/approval date
  
  Account                          Debit      Credit
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Inventory Damage Expense         250
      Inventory                               250
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (To record damaged inventory)


Entry 4: INTER-BRANCH TRANSFER
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Scenario: Transfer 10 units (cost 50 NGN each) from Branch A to Branch B

Entry F: Record Transfer
  Date: Transfer date
  
  Account                          Debit      Credit
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Inventory - Branch B             500
      Inventory - Branch A                   500
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (To record inter-branch inventory transfer)


================================================================================
                    MONTH-END ACCOUNTING PROCEDURES
================================================================================

At the end of each month, perform:

1. RECONCILE INVENTORY
   - Compare perpetual records with physical count
   - Record adjustments for shrinkage/variances
   - Investigation of major discrepancies

2. ACCRUE PAYABLES
   - Review outstanding invoices
   - Record accrued but unpaid expenses
   - Verify supplier statements

3. ACCRUE RECEIVABLES
   - Review unpaid invoices (if B2B)
   - Provide for doubtful debts if applicable

4. CLOSE REVENUE/EXPENSE ACCOUNTS
   - Transfer all revenue/expense accounts to retained earnings
   - Verify total revenue = total cash + accounts receivable - refunds
   - Verify total COGS = beginning inventory + purchases - ending inventory

5. GENERATE FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
   - Trial Balance
   - Balance Sheet
   - Income Statement
   - Cash Flow Statement
